Zhangjiajie – Huangshixi

Zhangjiajie – Huangshixi

Aug 7th, 2026

Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, Wulingyuan, Hunan Province, China

1080m

Nestled in the heart of China’s southern interior is an ethereal mountain landscape. While China’s south may be believed to flat, humid, and hot, This place proves one of the three wrong. As China’s first National Forest Park, Zhangjiajie is certainly a sight to behold. The main attraction of the park, the Zhangjiajie scenic area, is a natural reserve spanning 4810 hectares with an elevation difference of 900m throughout. Towering sandstone pillars up to 300m tall dot the landscape, formed by unique erosion patterns. Growing both between and on the mountains, lush jungles full of abundant flora provide a habitat to a variety of animals.

Through many instagram posts, reels, and all sorts of media I was exposed to Zhangjiajie. Not a particularly niche destination given its striking scenery and vast popularity. Being where the movie “Avatar” was filmed also provides an additional incentive to go (not for me, at least). But regardless of wether or not you liked the movie the landscape is something beautiful. Looking at pictures and videos of the place, it genuinely looks unreal. On good days, clouds float in between the pillars, really given them that cut off “floating island” look.

Zhangjiajie was on my bucketlist, and I was really motivated to visit it someday, I didn’t know when. In addition, I knew the trip would be easier for me to plan since I was Chinese, could speak the language, and would be going to China eventually to visit family.

After my long trip in Japan, my next destination was to be China. However, unlike Japan I didn’t really have too set of a plan on where I wanted to go. I knew for sure I was to land in my hometown Tianjin to visit family, then go to Chongqing to visit friends but that left at least 4 days in between where I could choose where I wanted to go. Feeling pretty drained after along time in Asia (aswell as the heat) I initially just felt like staying in Tianjin and taking it easy with family. During a talk with my uncle, I was asked what locations in China I would want to go to. I mentioned Zhangjiajie as the premier destination (i didn’t really know of too many other places). Unbeknownst to me, my uncle actually used to be a tour guide, and the next day he called back, asking to confirm if I wanted to go. Knowing that I would be in Chongqing, he made a plan for me to go to Zhangjiajie on the way back from the South. I felt really spoiled in this moment especially since he offered to pay for the entire expense too. The part I was most excited about was that I would be solo (first time solo travelling). However, alot of the logistics would be handled by him. (spoiled again) He was able to use his “tour guide connections” to get hotel, train, and park tickets for me relatively cheap.

With the logistics settled, my dream of visiting Zhangjiajie would be fulfilled with minimal effort from my end (thanks Uncle).

After spending time in Chongqing with my mother, brother, and 2 other friends I would depart by myself and head to Zhangjiajie via high speed rail (bullet train). Getting onto the train was relatively straightforward, and for my 2 day trip my only luggage would be some money and the clothes in my bag. The bullet train was comfortable, and I was able to catch a nap aswell as enjoying some of the spectacular views outside of the window. Chongqing was pretty mountainous, and the train would cut under mountains via tunnel as it headed towards Zhangjiajie.

I arrived after around 3 hours (very fast) and the next goal was to make it to the hotel. I was searching for a DD (chinese uber) when an uncle next to me asked if I needed a ride. Not wanted to get finissed, I busted out the chinese and asked if he could match the DD price. He could do it even cheaper, so I agreed. What I didn’t know was that he was no ordinary driver… The mode of transportation was not car, but motorbike. While I was surprised when I followed him out and saw the bike instead of the car, I still decided to go. Reason 1, The price WAS cheaper. Reason 2, I did want to experience the motorbike. In china, alot of transport for food delivery and whatnot is done via bike and I hadn’t tried that yet. With there being no seatbelt, I had to hold on tightly to a metal pipe on the bottom of the bike. The speed on the highway was fast enough that I could feel it in my eyes slightly. It was definitely a better experience than if I was on car, however, I was open to the air and could already see the pillar formations around me as we drove towards the town. While a little bit scary, it was definitely an experience to remember. Once we got there, I paid the driver, hopped off, and started walking to my hotel.

On the way to the hotel, I stopped by the river to check it out. There was some steps that you could use to walk over it, and I headed to the middle to take some photos. After this, I found the hotel, checked in, and dropped my stuff off.

I had the afternoon to waste so I decided to go on a walk in the town, checking out the stores and if there was anything for me to eat tonight. It’s worth mentioning that the main city is called Zhangjiajie, but the Zhangjiajie Park (place with all the pillars) is actually not in the main city, but located right next to a town north of Zhangjiajie called Wulingyuan. This was where I was staying. I stumbled past some street food stalls, but opted not to get any today. The streets were relatively clean and I found a place that offered some pretty plain Chinese food. My stomach definitely needed a break after Chongqing.

After some more walking and buying of some snacks, I went back to my hotel and got ready to go to sleep. My park ticket for tommorow was a really early time (630) and would be from the South gate. The Temple gate I mentioned earlier was the east one. This meant that I couldn’t just walk to enter the park, and would instead have to take a DD to the south gate to enter. I guess theres not much complaining about the gates as the ticket was free and it must’ve been the most convinient one for my uncle to book. The hotel room was fairly comfortable and the AC was a nice change from the heat outside.

I woke up the next morning feeling fairly refreshed and grabbed my stuff to head out and call an uber. While I was waiting outside for the uber to come, I found some foreginers trying to figure it out aswell. They asked me for help and were surprised when I responded in English. Instead of trying to order another one for them, we decided to all go in the one I ordered and split the bill later. They were 2 women, one from New Zealand and the other from Germany. The drive to the gate would weave us up and down through valleys, and the sight of the sunrise through the uniquely formed mountains was a sight to behold.

I arrived at the South Gate on time and was able to check in easily. We weren’t able to split the bill then and there but exchanged emails in order to get it done later. After saying bye to the pair, I headed off by myself towards the park. After the gate, there was a large statue. Being curious, I walked over and read it. Wu Guangzhong (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Guanzhong), a chinese painter who “discovered” Zhangjiajie. He wasn’t an explorer per se, but instead had travelled to Zhangjiajie, was enthralled by it’s beauty, and made many landscape drawings of the scenery. His paintings and subsequent essay on the park’s beauty popularized it and had resulted in the park being what it is today. Essentially he made it mainstream and popular to the public. Well, thank you for leading me to this awesome place!

I had done a little bit of planning before, and my general plan this day was to hike the “Golden Whip Stream” as I heard it was less crowded and more laid back. After walking for around 10 minutes past the gates I reached the “Grand Oxygen Square”. I honestly don’t know why it was named that, but it was cool regardless. It was essentially a grand plaza before entering the main park area with spectacular views of some sandstone pillars. This place was majestic, and the mist in the early morning shrouded the base of the pillars, making it look even more ethereal. Surrounding the square was also a little pond and a field of grass.

After spending some time to take in the views, I decided to continue on. I had done some planning before, and saw some reviews that the “Golden Whip Stream” was the best place to go if one wanted to avoid crowds. Apparently it’s a path that follows a stream in a valley of the park, winding through the bottoms of the pillars with some sick views. I had originally planned on hiking this and then figuring out where to go after. After some more walking, I reached a crossroads. I could choose to continue to go on the Golden Whip Stream, or go up some stairs to a place called “Huangshi Village”. Being the elevation chaser I was, I chose the village. I really didn’t know what to expect of a “village” just up some stone steps. With the stone path being moss covered and decrepit, I initially thought that it would be some super niche little attraction, but I would end up being wrong. On the entire way up, I only saw another pair of people.

Hiking here was comparable to the hike I did in Nara, Japan, but even more humid and hot. Before I knew it, I was sweating buckets even though I wasn’t very tired. The sounds of cicadas, crickets, and all sorts of other bugs around me also added some very nice ambience. I was more and more beginning to think that this place was super niche because there was almost no one around me. I stumbled upon a little cave formation and decided to take a rest. Here was where I saw my first monkey of the park. Surprisingly, this monkey was not very scared of me, even deciding to sit down and have a scratch. I was able to get pretty close and snap a nice photo

After spending a while in the cave, I decided to continue up. From here, the landscape was pretty similar to before, but with alot more breaks in the trees. The view kept on getting better and better and I was gaining alot more elevation than I initially expected. I thought it would just be a small hike up to an attraction but it was shaping out to be some sort of actual peak.

I continued grinding upwards and now I definitely believed that there was something big near the top. I could hear people yelling and talking, and the infrastructure around was getting increasingly developed. The views from here were great aswell. On my way up, I passed by a troop of monkey that were using the plastic “fence” to walk on the side of the path.

I passed by a couple of workers sweeping the path of leaves, and after 5 more minutes I arrived at Huangshixi. From the signs I deduced that Huangshixi was both the name of the peak and the village on top of it. At that point I was definitely glad that I went on this “niche” path up instead of just going on the golden whip stream. What further confirmed my feelings was a sign with a quote saying “One can not say they visited Zhangjiajie without visiting Huangshixi”. That was a nice bit of motivation that I was on the right track with my trip. For a tourist attraction village on the top of a peak, there was quite alot of different buildings and whatnot. There was a cool “temple” where alot of others were taking photos, aswell as gift shops and convinience stores. I refueled on water, took a quick break from the heat, and decided to continue on exploring the village.

This peak was alot larger than I initially realized. There was even a trail that basically “circumcised” it going around the top and it was 5km. This was more of a plateau vs a peak. Walking a little bit along the trail, I reached this lookout called the “Star Picking Terrace”. It was built on a small natural pillar and I had to take a ladder in order to go up. Luckily there was noone else here at this early in the morning so I was able to get some nice photos and enjoy the view all to myself.

I spent some more time really just taking in the scale of the pillars. It’s just kind of hard to imagine these huge freestanding sandstone structures jutting out of the landscape with whole trees growing on them. From here I left the terrace and headed to the temple to check it out. There were definitely ALOT more people on the peak now. What confused me was how. On the path up, I barely saw anyone else and there was quite alot of people. Looking at the map, I realized that there was a cableway going from another trail up to the peak. That’s where most of the people were coming from. Being a peakbagger I was glad I took the “real” way up. The temple was pretty typical, just a beautiful little building with alot of people taking photos. I checked out the map for the “circumcision” trail and saw quite a few viewpoints with exotic names, and decided that I needed to see what inspired these names.

I continued forward on the trail, wanting to make the most of my time. In addition to the stone stairs and path, there were also wooden platforms at the viewpoints that you could stand on. As you can see in the map, you could choose many different paths of different elevations around the trail to see the views from different perspectives. The amount of people was really becoming apparent and I could see that Huangshixi truly was quite a popular destination and not some below the radar place like I initially thought. I tried to keep a quick pace, but still made time to enjoy the stunning views. I could see why there were so many different names for the views now. All of the sandstone pillars were vastly different and each one was able to sort of “convey” a feeling or a scene. In particular, there was one formation where the sandstone pillars were arranged neatly in descending order, kind of looking like a dragon’s back. Every single formation was special, and the individual pillars never got old.

I continued along the trail, making sure to stop and admire the pillars at every viewpoint. The weather right now was quite pleasant, as there was fairly strong wind blowing in. The trail was well designed as well, feeling kind of like a museum gallery with platforms to stand at all of the viewpoints.

The pillars here were much larger than the ones viewed from the grand oxygen square. While they might look pretty typical once you compare their size with the full grown trees growing on top you can see the scale of these structures. The widths of the pillars varied too. Some looked spindly while others looked like full on plateaus that you could build a village on. Compared to a typical mountain, the sheerness of the bare cliff faces really gave the pillars are sense of grandeur that cant really be described. I could just imagine how many years of erosion cut into the rock, forming those huge faces.

Continuing on the trail I encountered a curious site. Lying on one of the platforms was an ordinary housecat. Lots of people were taking photos of the cat and it seemed like it was having a good time enjoying itself in the sun. From what I saw, the cat had no collar and it would make no sense for someone to bring their cat into the park to sunbathe. The best guess I had was that it was a cat that belonged to the village keepers and who liked to roam around the park. This was a pretty cool sight among the grand sandstone pillars.

Something I liked aswell was the informational signs along the path. They described local flora, fauna, their origins, aswell as some of the geological processes that formed the Zhangjiajie pillars in the first place. They were a nice break from the landscape and also gave me some new information about the ecology around me.

The monkeys now were becoming more and more common. There were monkeys by themselves aswell as groups of them moving around. Th emotive seemed to be the same, however and that was to get food from the tourists nearby. They were pretty tame, but you needed to be careful to hide your food away when they got close.

A little break into the trail came in the form of a bridge. It looked pretty “cute” and was around 40m across a river ravine. The bottom was a grate so you could stand over it and look into the slits to the valley floor. The ravine was very deep and gave me a better sense of how steep and rugged the mountains/cliffs truly were.

There were alot more of the educational signs as I went along. They all provided some useful information about the life and the park. I will try to include some of the ones I found interesting in the photos. In particular, I saw one describing the special adaptations that the trees/shrubs had to grow on the side and tops of the sandstone pillars, aswell as the special nature of the rock. The pillars were made out of a special kind of sandstone called “quartz sandstone” and in it’s formation would develop cracks. The sandstone was porous and would retain water in it, and dirt would accumulate into cracks, allowing sandstone to grow. The trees that grew on the pillars also needles, maximizing moisture retention.

I took LOTS of photos each time I stopped, but definitely not going to upload them all. The formations never got boring, and I could see the “themes” of each viewpoint that inspired it’s name. The scale is much more visible when you focus on the sizes of the trees growing on top. In particular, there was one plateau shaped kind of like an elephant. The trunk was connected to the main body via a natural arch and the eye was the shadow created by said arch. The formations never cease to amaze. Also, the monkeys constantly running back and forth on the plastic railings added alot of playful ambience.

The spacing, heights, and thickness all combined together to create a sort of “theme”. The views were spoiling me at this point.

After some while of continuing along the trail, I spotted quite a large crowd that had formed in front of me. Walking over, I discovered a huge group of people surrounding 2 monkeys. Upon looking further, I discovered that it was actually 4 monkeys and not 2. It was a mother-baby pair. The baby monkeys were holding onto the chest area of the mother monkeys and had their arms wrapped around their torso. They were just kind of sitting in the middle of the path, as tourists threw snacks to them. What was funny to me was how the mother monkeys would pick up all the snacks and eat them, not giving any to their baby. Maybe there was some sort of monkey-baby feeding relationship that I wasn’t familiar with. Another monkey came over, trying to get some of the snacks but one of the mothers scared them off. The baby monkeys also detatched sometimes and would just sit and look cute. Just a note: Many people were carrying sticks sold by vendors. These were used to hit the monkeys when they became aggressive.

After taking quite alot of photos and walking past the gaggle of tourists, I continued back into the main village area of Huangshixi. The trail went around in a big loop around the plateau and brought me back the the village area. My stomach was still feeling a little bit funny after Chongqing, so I headed to the washroom quickly. It was suprisingly clean for a public toilet in China, but I guess that expected of a tourist destination in China. Luckily, I had decided to bring my own toilet paper which was a good idea as there was none provided. After that, I decided to explore some other small side trails near the village which I hadn’t seen yet. Walking through the forest paths was beautiful especially with the morning sun coming out. I also saw some beautiful looking trees on a plateau that looked straight out of a painting. You could see how the trees were growing on the little dirt clumps on the rock – just like how the information sign described.

At this point, I was looking at the map to see where I wanted to go next. I would have to backtrack (wasting precious time) to get to the Golden whip stream so I instead opted to continue forward deeper into the park. I realized that the only way to go forward and not back to where I can from was going to be via cable car. Only one hiking path went up Huangshixi and it was in the direction I came from. So I decided to cough up the around 30 yuan (6 dollars) for the cable car down. I wanted to experience it at least once anyways and I had already climbed up here by myself. This price was SIGNIFICANTLY more reasonable than the sky pilot one (100 dollars) and would save me alot of time. The view was great from the cable car aswell. I was an amazing experience almost “floating” down Huangshixi through the mountains.

I made it to the bottom. From the picture of the map that I had on my phone, I decided that the next destination I wanted to go to was Yuanjiajie. It was another mountain village/peak like Huangshixi and would be a nice objective to tick off.

I was really glad that I decided to go to Huangshixi instead of continuing along the Golden Whip Stream. In hindsight, I definitely should’ve done more research but alls well that ends well. For my first few hours in the park, it was great. It was hot, but managable due to the wind and shade. I would’ve wanted to see the “cloud sea” between the pillars but having a clear view was definitely appreciated aswell. Zhangjiajie was definitely living up to my expectations. I liked the experience of hiking up and then walking leisurely to enjoy the views. There was a bus loop at the bottom, and I started looking for the bus to take me to Yuanjiajie.
