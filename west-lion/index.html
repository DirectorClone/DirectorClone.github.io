West Lion

The West Lion

June 25th, 2026

Lions Bay, British Columbia

1654m

Almost everyone living in Vancouver has seen the twin peaks known as the Lions. From most of the lower mainland, it is clearly visible and is an iconic landmark of the city with many places named after them (Lions bay, lions gate bridge, etc.) While beautiful from afar, the real fun comes in from the class 3-4 scramble up to the summit.

While I knew of the Lions, prior to this year I never knew that there was a (somewhat mainstream) trail leading up to the base. When I went up Thomas peak, with my buddy Mark we had tried to summit the Lion from there (much more technical and difficult without fixed rope) and unfortunately failed due to ice and bad weather. So when I later found out that there was a much easier path up I knew I had to send it. This path would lead us to the base of the other side of the Lion where there apparently was some fixed rope.

For my partners I asked David and Jake to tag along, both cool dudes whose cardio I knew would be good so we wouldn’t have to take too many breaks. They hadn’t done scrambling of this level before but they were confident after looking at some pictures and videos. (David in particular needed more pushing) The trail although acessible was quite long so we wanted to move fast. This trail was also the same one that split into Harvey and Brunswick so it was familiar territory for the first stretch. David’s parent picked me and Jake up and we were able to get to the trailhead around 6 in the morning. We waited a while for Jake to stretch and then plodded forward onto the logging road. The weather looked okay, a little cloudy as we plodded up the gravel switchback that is the logging road.

We continued trodding forwards on the old road and split off twice from tunnel bluffs/ brunswick, and then past the harvey turn around. It was much flatter here, much more comfortable for us. We were feeling pretty good so we decided to take a little break at the harvey turn around to eat some snacks and talk. From here we continued forward to a little bench/viewpoint that I remembered from last time. To our surprise there was an offroad vehicle here with no one inside. We suspected that it was a park ranger who parked their vehicle hear and went on ahead to mark the trail. (suspicions confirmed by brand new fresh trail markers later on) We continued on the flat logging, passing a nice waterfall and stream where we refilled our bottles.

The logging road started getting rockier and rockier with some significant elevaiton until we got to the splitoff towards harvey creek. Honestly was never sure where the trail ahead of us even went. We turned right and descended 150m down to the creek and crossed the bridge. From here it was continuous typical mountain terrain up and up through the forest. We saw some big reishi mushrooms, and had some fun throwing rocks at some tall one. I told David not to throw while we were nearby but he was confident in his skills. The rock hit the tree, flung backwards and nearly nailed Jake in the head but he jumped back and it missed him by a bit.

Halfway up to the ridge we got to a bouldery section. There were some scree spots and a little traverse across the boulders before the ridge. The elevation gain here was alot worse than before but the change in scenery motivated us. We continued on and finally reached the ridge. There was a flat spot where we rested and took some photos. We were talking too…. It looked like a nice spot to camp out with beautiful views of Harvey and unnecessary. Some other guys clearly had the same idea that we did, as we saw alot of burnt out campfire pits (like 3) We could finally see the West Lion at this point.

We continued up the ridge for a while, now completely out of treeline. We traversed around a boulder field where we got slightly off trail but were able to find the markers. From here it was just more and more boulders. There was a steepish section of boulders and we spotted a little shortcut so we decided to straight up instead of looping around onto the ridge. This part was pretty fun and after hiking up these huge rock formations we were face to face with the Lion.

We finally reached the Lion massif and from here on it was just send. We continued forward and there were plenty of cliff drops and exposure all around. The fixed line was helpful and we went down one by one onto the hump before doing the climb up.

From here all we had to do was to climb this big rock and then make it to the top. The first part was very exposed, especially on the hump we were on before we even made it to the climb. We climbed up onto this slab on the left, and then continued on climber’s right. This part felt really exposed as it was straight drop to our right but the fixed chain helped alot. Very nice to grab onto. After this there was a 40-50 degree slab with a fixed rope. You could’ve avoided using the rope if u stuck to the left or the right and kept on going up but we decided to use the rope because why not. From here it was just more fun scrambling. I would say class 3 for me with some class 4 moves. All of the holds were solid but there was some thinking involved on which hold to grab. Definitely test before you put your weight on it. It was definitely more exposed than anything I’d been on before as it was straight drop behind us. Once you make it to the part where you have to fit through the trees you know you are about halfway up the boulder. Entire thing is a no fall zone except for the tree part.

From here it was just more scramble, but I would say the second half is much easier than the first and felt less exposed. We continued forward without much difficulty from here. The final section is some loose scree but is very gentle grade. We made it to the top of the boulder finally. Felt really good standing up there. The clouds were starting to roll in but the view was still very nice. We were able to see as far as Garibaldi.

It was genuinely freezing at the top. Jake didn’t have a jacket but even with mine it was very cold. We stayed for maybe 15 minutes at the top to eat all of our food – would’ve stayed longer but it was wayyyyy too cold. We started the descent. It was a little harder and took longer, especially for David but was still very fun. We got to the hump, climbed back up the rope, and then traversed back the way we came across the rocks and boulders. We took some breaks but it didn’t take too long for us to reach treeline again. The clouds were really starting to roll in and it was cool watching them billow over the ridge.

We went into treeline and took it slower on the descent since Jake and mine’s knees were feeling it. It was all chill from here except for the part where we had to ascend back from the bridge up to the logging road. Going down the logging road was nice and we had some very insightful conversations on the way down. We refilled water at the waterfall.

We kept on going and were almost at the car before out first “accident” happened. We were taking the missing link trail which was already a shortcut from the 2nd switchback to the parking lot but Jake had the idea of just descending straight down the hill through the foliage. It sounded good as it wasn’t particularly steep and I didn’t care at this point about getting my pants dirty. So we went down while David stayed on the normal trail. Halfway down this “shortcut” I suddenly heard Jake yell and looked up from my pathfinding (jake was in front of me). This guy dropped both his poles and immediately started running/ jumping / falling down the mountain while dirt slid behind him. It honestly was kinda sick, looked like he was escaping a dirt avalanche of sorts. The reason why he did that was because he had stepped on a hornets nest and got stung around 5 times. I was far behind so they didn’t aggro onto me, but me and David had to retreive his poles as Jake was long gone to the parking lot. We grabbed our poles and basically used them as hooks to retreive his.

The hornets were buzzing all around the handles of his poles, definitely on his scent. After this we gave the nest a wide berth and continued down to the parking lot. There we discovered Jake being treated by Davids mom with some sort of ointment, and we also found out that one of the hornets actually followed him inside the car and stung him again on the face. Apparently the hornet was called the bald faced hornet and especially make their nests on the forest floor. He was fine but he said it was very painful on the car ride home.

I remember seeing this beautiful mountain at the top of the Lion, and researched it, it was called Sky Pilot. That was sure to be the next objective.
