Sky Pilot Mountain

Sky Pilot Mountain

July 6th, 2026

Squamish, British Columbia

2031m

Between the North Shore mountains and the stunning peaks of Garibaldi prov park lies an iconic jagged massif with the tallest peak being Sky Pilot mountain. Located near the Stawamus Chief, it’s prominence over the Shannon valley and pocket glacier makes it a popular intro mountaineering objective.

After ascending the West Lion last weekend, Sky Pilot seemed like a perfect next objective. On every summit panorama it was easily the most iconic peak in the near vicinity. The weather for Sunday looked great so we decided to send it after reading a few trip reports and looking at some instagram posts. (Thank you janny ikigai and steven song)

Originally the team included me, Jake, and David, but after some mishaps we were unable to reach David. After some rocks thrown at his window, we just decided to go and reached the Sea to Sky parking lot around 930. We were originally supposed to get there at 8 but whatever. The weather looked absolutely amazing, but was a tad hot. The actual Sky Pilot Valley Trail starts at the top of the sea to sky gondola (800m) , but after looking at the prices (80 dollars is crazy) we just decided to hike up. It was wooded but we were still able to snag in some views of squamish aswell as the chief.

After maybe an hour or so of heavy vertical through forest and rope we made it to a rock outcrop where we could see the gondola aswell as our first view of sky pilot.

From here we trudged forwards towards the gondola ableit with much less elevation gain. Met alot of hikers on the way up and had some cool conversations too. We got to the gondola around 1130 and decided to take a little stroll and break. Food looked nice but was very overpriced so we just grabbed some condiments to refuel aswell as a nice bathroom break.

From here we were able to finally start the sky pilot trail. We left the gondola and descended down a gravel road towards the trail. From here, there would be little elevation gain as we walked through the valley until we got to the actual base of sky pilot.The flat gravel was so nice compared to the painful logging roads at the start of the Lions Bay trails. We made quick pace, eventually entering the forest where we saw some campers returning and a family of grouse.

Continuing on for an hour of flat trail we finally got to a rockier part with some moderate elevation gain. The sun was really getting to us at this point so we decided to take a quick break to eat some food. Contuing on, the “official” path ended but we continued to follow the well marked path up through the forest. Elevation gain here was pretty considerable. We passed shannon creek, where we also refilled our bottles. After some more hard elevation, we made it to a rocky basin that we had to trudge up before the glacier. We took another break before heading up. Honestly, this was the worst part of the entire trip due to the steep slope and scree. Was very annyoing to crawl up but we did it regardless after ditching the poles near a rock. Views of Habrich behind us were opening up too.

Continuing up the scree we passed a few waterfalls and finally made it to the stadium glacier. Axes out, I put on my crampons but Jake decided to ditch his and just follow my steps up. A change of scenery in the snow was nice and we followed the glacier up until we got to a steep “wall”. From here, we decided to take the right path which looked easier but just with a slightly longer traverse. Max snow angle was around 50 degrees.

The steep snow wasn’t so bad as it was hard with lots of bootprints. We just followed the guys on the right up while being careful with our steps and axe placements. Halfway up i turned to my right and spotted some climbers going up a different path to the Copilot ridge. I watched one climber try to transition from steep snow to a rock ledge, and suddenly he lost balance and slipped off. It was horrific as he dropped his axe immediately and sommersaulted 5 times over what must’ve been a 50 meter slide. When he stopped rolling I called out to him and he was responsive. He said his arm was broken and we asked if he needed SAR but he said he would just continue on. We saw his 2 buddies climbing down so we just continued on. Coming onto the ridge we packed up our snow gear and finally reached the infamous pink slab. There were lots of climbers rappelling down so we had to wait a bit. Honestly, the climb was not bad at all. I would say the whole thing was class 3-4 scrambling and I didn’t feel particularly exposed. After finishing the slab, we continued along the ridge with a few other climbers.

We continued to traverse along the ridge, and were met back with my worst enemy (scree). The exposure wasn’t particularly bad, but on the overhang traverses i always kept 2 hands on the rock. The views were amazing on the traverse to the summit. We reached the 2 gullies and went up. There were a few class 5 moves but I never felt particularly challenged or exposed. This was harder than the pink slab.

After a tiny more traverse we were almost there, just had to do some route finding along a boulder field before we reached the summit. We summited around 330 and were met with a pleasant surprise. 2 of the guys we saw near the parking were also there. It was nice talkign with them again. We ate some food, had a little photoshoot, and just enjoyed the spectacular view from the top.

After spending maybe 30 minutes or so at the top we decided to head back down. Some slow climbers slowed us down and we also had to wait for another rappel down the pink slab. We did bring rope but we felt confident on the pink slab so we just downclimbed the right side next to the rapellers (aura). The helicopter also finally came to take the other climber off the mountain. Once we reached the snow slope we decided to buttslide down. Originally we planned against it but it was too tempting. Both of us slid down the 2 sections (wet butts) and we packed up our gear to head down the scree. It was much easier heading down suprisingly and we also stopped by the waterfall to take a shower.

We met the 2 guys again but they blitzed us past the trail. From here we just watched our step and kept on heading down. Once we got to the valley we picked up pace as we wanted to get down fast before it started getting dark. We reached the gondola around 8 and immediately started heading down the vertical part again.

We continued down fast and it got dark in the middle of the descent which slowed us down. Slipped quite a bit and the dark woods was definitely not fun. We made it back in 1 piece but would definitely not do that wooded descent next time. Very jealous of the people in the gondola above us. We were soaked with sweat as we were hustling down fast but we made it to the parking lot in one piece.

We made it to the lot, got some water, and then started the drive home. It was soooo unforunate, but there was a huge crash that backed up the entire sea to sky highway. We ended up getting home at 3 am ….. crazy…. Me and Jake did talk a bit about the next objective, what sounded good was camping near the glacier, and then waking up early to hit the other peaks around skypilot (copilot, ledge) before heading back down. Sounds fun!
