Zhangjiajie – Tianzishan Day 2

Zhangjiajie – Tianzishan Day 2

August 7th-8th, 2026

Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, Wulingyuan, Hunan Province, China

1262m

Waking up the next day, I felt refreshed and ready to tackle the objective. The AC in the hotel was just right, and the bed was very comfortable aswell. I was supposed to check out this morning, so I had to pack everything into my bag and get ready to go. Packing was fairly straightforward, as I hadn’t brought much in the first place and as a result my bag was fairly light. My entry time today was not as early as before, so I had some more leeway in the morning. After finishing my packing, I decided to head down to check out the complementary breakfast. It was pretty tasty. While it was oily and fried (to be expected) there was a nice mix of a traditional continental western breakfast and oriental food that I was more used to.

After eating breakfast, I headed out. The weather was nice, it wasn’t as hot as I was expecting actually. I would be entering from the East Gate today (the one in the park) so I wouldn’t have to take any DiDi, I could just walk straight over. Retracing my steps from yesterday, I walked along the river, making my way to the gate. The views, even from the town were absolutely beautiful. The mountains all around me, while not the sandstone pillars, had some crazy jut. I believe these were the early stages in the formation of the sandstone pillars. Geology sure is beautiful.

It didn’t take me too long until I got to the east gate and checked in. This time, feeling refreshed, I opted to not take the sightseeing train (one experience is enough) and I decided to hike up the 10 mile gallery instead.

Hiking up the gallery was pretty fun. The incline was very light, and due to the path literally being paved, it was more of just a walk up. It was also pretty funny watching the sightseeing trains roll pass me. They all had funny engineheads. Because I wasn’t in the train, I was also able to stop and properly look around to enjoy the views.

It was very nice and peaceful, especially since there wasn’t alot of other people on the trail (minus the trains rolling past me every minute)

I got to the “mini village” at the bottom of Tianzishan pretty quickly. The heat was definitely getting to me at this point, so I decided to take a little break. I sat down for a bit, ate some snacks, and continued on. Apparently there was some sort of “monkey garden” here that I missed the day before so I decided to check that out aswell.

From here, I turned onto the Tianzishan trail. The exact same route that I took to descend last time. It was nice, just being a very hot, humid stairmaster. It felt like Huangshixi again, being a nice, lonely path covered by trees. I was able to get some glimpses between the dense vegetation and snag some nice photos.

Halfway up, the trees started thinning out and I was able to get some much nicer views of the surrounding structures. In particular, there was this one plateau that stuck out. It seemed to be another “early stage” of the pillars with a large rock face not completely worn out, but still showing lots of erosion. Basically a chunk of the earth with a rugged cliff.

Continuing on, I encountered some people heading up and going down. Looks like they didn’t want to pay for the gondola either. Relatable. There were lots of cool rock structures next to me that looked like fun scrambles, but I definitely wouldn’t trust my weight on sandstone… Granite only.

I started seeing little buildings around me that I remembered from yesterday, so I knew I was near the top. These little structures really added a cool vibe to the entire place. They made it really feel like I was exploring some village (minus the people). The worn downness also made it more authentic aswell.

I was definitely getting close to the village now, maybe I was already in it actually. There were lots more buildings and lots more people, but most of the buildings were just rest spots. There was also an archery station which was funny. I hadn’t noticed that yesterday.

Going up, up, up and I was at the village now. It was crowded around the same as yesterday. Alot more than Huangshixi, but that’s just because I was up there so early. I was feeling very hot, and very hungry, so I decided to sit down and enjoy a banana. The drinks and snacks up here looked pretty appetizing aswell. One of the stores in front of me was also sellling tie dyed apparel which was advertised in the tie dyed decorations around me.

I got up and decided to keep on moving around and check out the formations around Tianzishan. There was a hiking path around the mountain village, kinda like Huangshixi, but smaller. I decided to go around that to finish my time at Tianzishan. I was not dissapointed. The views here were beautiful, with crazy formations. It seems like they just keep on getting better.

I wandered around the village a little more, checking out the different structures and the wide array of things that were being sold. It was getting pretty bustling now.

Apparently there was some sort of attraction nearby called “The No. 1 Bridge in the world” I looked at the map and saw that I could check it on on my descent towards the golden whip stream. My plan now was basically to check out the views on the way to the “bridge”, then check out the bridge, and then descend to the golden whip stream and call it a day. Sounded pretty good.

It turned out that the No. 1 Bridge was not a man made bridge like I thought, but instead it was a natural arch that could be walked on. This was alot more cool than I thought. However, there was ALOT of people. This was the most crowded part of the park so far. I had to walk super slowly and it was basically single file on the entire bridge.

I got on the bridge, and (painfully) crossed over onto the other pillar. I opted not to go up after crossing, as it looked like it would’ve taken quite a long time with all the people. Standing over the arch was good enough. I turned around, and crossed back to the main area.

After leaving the bridge, I continued moving along the Tianzishan trail and checking out the views. The plateau stood out again, and it was definitely very interesting how flat and even the tops were. Some of the plateaus even looked big enough to build more villages…

I continued on, walking through an area with a little pond in it in order to reach the next viewpoint.

I had basically seen everything on Tianzishan, so I decided to descend and head down to the Golden whip stream. There were 2 paths up and down the mountain so I would be taking the one that I hadn’t taken before. I reached the path, and apparently it was under construction but only a part of it. After a little bit of talking with the workers, they let me through. They were fixing up this one part of the path but the rest looked pretty intact, basically the same as everything else. The good thing was that due to the construction at the start of the path, not alot of people went this way so I basically had the trail all to myself. It was a very nice change to be hiking quietly again after all the people at the No. 1 Bridge.

The added benefit of there not being so much people was the wildlife. I saw a monkey troop pass by, I guess they also liked the more niche places. The monkeys were nice and well behaved aswell.

There was a little dirt side path next to the main trail so I decided to explore that for a bit and check it out. There was nothing much, just a small pond, but it sure was peaceful.

I continued on the main trail and was met with more views of sandstone pillars. It really just never gets old. I also met European family who I talked to aswell. There were a surprisingly large amount of European tourists, but not alot of Americans at all from what I’ve seen. Maybe the views of China in Europe were different from the views of China in North America.

Suprisingly, alltrails does work in China, so I had been using that for navigation in addition to the physical poster maps around the park. Apparently there was a waterfall up ahead (according to alltrails so I was pretty excited for that). There was a waterfall, streaming off the side of one of the cliffs but due to the way it was positioned it was kinda hard to see. However, it was quite loud which made it pretty noticable. I couldn’t really see the bottom but I assumed that the water fell all the way to the valley floor, which would’ve made it quite a large waterfall considering the elevation it started from. Continuing forward, I also discovered a little closed off cave/ arch thing. I knew I had to check it out so I ducked under the barrier and headed over. The trail was fine, but it was covered in a lot of leaves which meant I had to watch my step to be careful not to slip. There were some pretty nice views from the bottom of the “cave” but it was nothing too special compared to everything else.

I headed back up and continued making my way around the trail. This part was all still relatively flat and my energy levels still felt great although I was very tired. I saw some more lone monkeys, and managed to get some pretty nice close pictures. There was another little waterfall but instead of streaming down the entire cliffside, this one collected in a little pool next to it. Usually, I would love to drink alpine water, but this was definitely something different….

I think this was the most amount of monkeys I’ve seen in the park. Maybe it was due to the combined factors of 1. Not being alot of people on the trail and 2. the perfect time of day? Nevertheless I was seeing either single ones or entire families around every 10 minutes. They were a slight distraction to, as I would stop for each monkey and take photos… I was passing some more of the little structures like on the way to Tianzishan, but this time was different. I could definitely tell that this trail was much less used and thus also much less serviced. All the buildings were run down, rusted, and falling apart. I found a washroom aswell, and it was super dirty and unsanitary. In addition, the trash cans were all overflowing and the ground next to them was strewn with trash. Definitely must’ve been the work of the monkeys aswell. It was pretty surprising how much the monkeys were interacted with in this environment. Typically in Canada it’s always about “leave no trace” so that animals don’t get used to human contact, but it was clearly very different here….

The trail was starting to descend alot more, and instead of looking at the pillars from above, I was now starting up at their cliffs from below. There truly were some grand rock walls, but the view was ruined a bit by the trash strewn around… I passed some more decripit houses. Really felt like I was exploring some abandoned village. From the way I was positioned relative to the pillars now, I was definitely getting close to the valley floor.

I was seeing some people coming up now, and after asking them I found out that I was only a few minutes away to the valley. The views here were really grand, and staring at the pillars from below give a much different feeling than looking at them from above.

I made it to the bottom, and while I didn’t feel too tired, my knees were definitely feeling the strain of descending. I felt pretty accomplished though, concluding my 2 day adventure on Tianzishan. In total, 1 ascent and 2 descents. The next objective was to be the golden whip stream, and my final experience in the park. It felt surreal that I was almost done already, but these 2 days had definitely felt accomplishing up to now.
