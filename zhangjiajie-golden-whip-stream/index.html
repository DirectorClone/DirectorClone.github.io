Zhangjiajie – Golden Whip Stream

Zhangjiajie – Golden Whip Stream

Aug 8th, 2026

Zhangjiajie National Forest Park, Wulingyuan, Hunan Province, China

600m

It felt like no time had passed at all between me hiking up to Tianzishan and back down. I guess time flies when you’re having fun. It was nice being in the valley, and a little bit sad that I wouldn’t be able to enjoy the pillars from elevation again (I wouldn’t be going up any more peaks) but of course, looking at the pillars from below was very grand and nice. I guess that’s just how it is, life needs to be enjoyed from a multitude of perspectives. After taking a small rest, I started hiking forward on the trail. My original first objective, now going to be my last… Hiking on flat ground was nice and alot easier than descending / ascending although it definitely felt less accomplishing.

Basically the trail would follow it’s namesake (the Golden Whip Stream/ Gorge) occasionally crossing it via some bridges. It was not a very large river, and although there were some deep parts alot of it was very wadable. I did see some adults and children playing by the river aswell. Looked fun.

Although the stream was very small now, perhaps it was a contributer to the formation of the sandstone pillars. I don’t think that it was a coincidence that the river valley was surrounded on every side by the pillars. Perhaps the ancient version of the Golden Whip Stream had carved out this valley.

Walking along this trail was very peaceful. Even though now was definitely a nicher time, there was surprisingly not alot of people on the path at all. This vibe was definitely appreciated. The stream was starting to get alot deeper now and I even saw quite alot of fish. They were just swimming in place, against the current of the stream and their flashing bodies were a beautiful addition. I also saw some people with nets standing in the stream trying to fish some…. Fishing in a protected national park is crazy but I guess that’s just how it is here.

I continued on, making sure to leisurely enjoy the landscape around me. I wanted to make the most of my time in the park and also take advantage of how uncrowded it was right now.

I saw my first golden whip stream monkey here. These guys were alot more dynamic and agressive than the ones on the mountains. Maybe this was the distinction between lowland and mountain monkeys… I saw a whole family aswell, and this one mom in particular had alot of babies.

There was a part of the trail with what appeared to be a little cave thing. You could go around it aswell but I decided to go through for the experience of course… There was water dripping from the side of it, and it was very damp. It was also very cool in there, which was definitely a very nice change compared to the heat I had been in.

I made it to a little food stall / village area, and I decided to buy some water to drink as I had ran out of my water before. There were also some cute little cats here. I assume that they were owned by the shop owner as they looked very clean and well kept but also were very skittish aswell.

Walking forward some more, I made it to an area where some people were gathered around taking pictures of the water. Upon some further inspection, I realized that there was a Chinese Giant Salamander in the water. It was truly huge, and cool being able to see a salamander of that size. Apparently this species was the largest in the world. It’s camoflauge was pretty good, and it almost looked like a very weirdly shaped rock if you didn’t pay attention.

I made it to surprisingly large and well kept building at the trail, but it didn’t look like there was anything going on…. The views were still great and the afternoon sun really helped make everything look even more amazing.

Another attraction that I saw was the “stream of longevity”. It was basically a teeny tiny natural spring flowing out of a rock that had been marked with a sign. While it was cool, the actual amount of water flowing out was almost miniscule. The mouth of the spring was shaped like a dragon, I didn’t know that those generated naturally….

I was nearing the end of the trail, but I also was feeling pretty tired, hot, and sweaty so I decided to just turn back and hike the other way out of the park. I looked at the map and this was the last major “attraction” that there was on the trail. I also wanted some leeway time to make sure I caught my flight. It would be pretty nice to get some shots of the pillars from a different angle aswell.

I was on my way back, when I encountered a group of monkeys. I walked pretty quickly passed them and one of them hissed at me, and jumped on a tree to swipe at my face. Good thing I backed up quickly otherwise he definitely would’ve gotten me… I wasn’t even carrying any food, these lowland monkeys were just plain violent.

I made it to the start of the golden whip stream (the end for me) and recognized the grand oxygen plaza from my first day ahead of me. I was very tired at this point so I kept on pushing forward, but I also made time to enjoy the beautiful scenery around me lit by the sun.

I made it, and standing there in the square, I took a moment to appreciate the beautiful landscape that I was lucky to be in. The same structures which captivated me on the first day were even more beautiful now after moving through the whole park.

Moving forward, I left the park using the same entrance that I had entered on the first day. I took a few last looks of the beautiful landscape around me, knowing that this was an experience I would never forget.

I also made sure to read the plaque at the entrance gate in front of the statue of Wu Guangzhong again. An artist who popularized Zhangjiajie, and was also the reason that I was able to experience the beauty of the park.

I made it to the exit lot, and booked a DiDi. I would have to get to the airport and catch my flight, and my next stop would be Tianjin directly so I wouldn’t really have a proper place to rest. I was pretty hungry so I was also hoping that I could buy some food at the airport terminal.

I made it to the airport and was able to check in relatively easily although I had to go through some extra trouble because of my Canadian passport. Sitting down, there wasn’t too much food around to buy so I decided to just eat some sausages I had bought earlier. I had nothing to open them with so I had to resort to my pencil sharpener…

I also met some other Tianjin residents while waiting for the plane (I could tell from the accent) and they handed me this snack to try. It was basically some sort of berry that you chew on then spit out. I liked it alot but they told me later that it actually was addictive. Oh well. I also met the Spanish tourist from yesterday, what a coincidence that he was leaving today aswell. He was going to Shanghai now. The plane was delayed for 2 hours but it’s whatever I just took some time to relax.

I boarded the plane, and I was finally able to relax properly after a long day.

I took some time to reflect on my journey. I was so grateful to my uncle especially for helping to plan this trip and fulfill one of my longtime dreams. The park truly was amazing, from the grand pillars, the lush vegetation, and the abundant wildlife, and it lived up to all my expectations. I was also happy that I was able to summit 3 peaks and get some nice peace and quiet on the side trails. Although I went in with very little planning, I was able to visit most if not all of the park in 2 days, having fun while not tiring myself out too much. I don’t ever think I’ll be able to have the same spectacular feeling of seeing the pillars for the same time. Seeing huge sandstone structures rising out of the ground for 200m was just crazy…. Zhangjiajie definitely was worth it, and I would 100 percent go back. I was super glad that I got to experience this, and I had a blast the entire time!
